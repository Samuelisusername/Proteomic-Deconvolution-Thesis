# Proteomic Deconvolution Thesis

Lymphoma is a type of blood cancer affecting the white blood cells, called lymphocytes, which play an important role in the human immune system.  
To analyze this disease, many RNA-based algorithms have been developed to estimate the cell composition of tumor environments. However, approximately one third of patients who develop drug resistance to a subtype of lymphoma exhibit a poor prognosis, with an expected overall survival of approximately a year and a half.

There remains a significant gap in accurate diagnosis and in choosing the right treatment strategy for individual patients.  
In this thesis, we demonstrate the efficacy and limitations of various **proteomic deconvolution methods** combined with different normalization strategies. Furthermore, we provide a testing framework that generates realistic synthetic data and evaluates proteomic profile estimations.

This framework serves as a foundation for developing and benchmarking future tools aimed at generating more accurate proteomic profile estimations. Improved accuracy in cell fraction estimation can ultimately contribute to advancing personalized treatment strategies for lymphoma patients.
